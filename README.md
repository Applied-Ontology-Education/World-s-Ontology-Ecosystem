# The World’s Ontology Ecosystem

This repository supports **The World’s Ontology Ecosystem**, a five-day intensive ontology course hosted by the University at Buffalo and the National Center for Ontological Research.

The course is designed for a mixed audience. Some participants are experienced ontology engineers; others are encountering applied ontology, OWL, and GitHub for the first time. You may participate by observing the sessions, completing the exercises, reviewing the work of others, or doing all three.

The course is led by **John Beverley** and **Barry Smith**, with contributions from researchers and practitioners working in government, industry, applied research, ontology engineering, and artificial intelligence.

## Course Approach

The central theme of the course is that producing ontology syntax is relatively easy. Producing ontology content that is clear, reusable, logically sound, standards-aligned, and fit for operational use is much harder.

Across the week, participants will practice an ontology-engineering tradecraft that combines:

- careful analysis before formalization;
- competency questions and explicit scope;
- the seven-bucket modeling strategy;
- distinctions between entities and information about them;
- RDF, RDFS, OWL, and automated reasoning;
- Semantic Web and software-engineering practices;
- ontology evaluation and validation;
- documentation, review, governance, and maintenance;
- critical evaluation of AI-assisted ontology engineering.

## Ways to Participate

### Observers

Observers may attend the talks, follow the exercise walkthroughs, inspect the course materials, and participate in GitHub Discussions.

Observers do **not** need to fork the repository or submit exercise files.

Observers are encouraged to:

- ask questions about the talks and exercises;
- respond to discussion prompts;
- examine participant models;
- comment on modeling decisions;
- suggest propositions for the daily debates;
- compare their own reasoning with the worked solutions.

### Builders

Builders complete the daily exercises individually or in groups.

Builders should:

1. Fork the repository.
2. Clone or download their fork.
3. Open the relevant daily exercise folder.
4. Read that day’s `README.md`.
5. Complete the associated exercises.
6. Commit and push the completed work to their fork.
7. Share a link in GitHub Discussions.

Builders should work at the level appropriate to their experience. Some exercises will include a guided path for beginners and optional extensions for participants with more experience in ontology, OWL, reasoning, or programming.

### Enrolled Students

Enrolled students follow the same exercise process as Builders.

They are additionally expected to:

- participate actively throughout the week;
- give a synthesis presentation on Friday.

The Friday presentation topics will be provided to each enrolled student.
