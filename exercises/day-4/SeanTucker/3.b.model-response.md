## Reasoning

2. **The Old Gold smoker is out.** He owns snails, and his nationality isn't given, so this exclusion is shared across everyone who could be him.
3. **The exclusion falls unevenly.**

## Assumptions (standard for the puzzle, but not stated in the ontology)

- Each attribute takes exactly one distinct value per house: five nationalities, five colours, five drinks, five brands, and five pets. This implies a fifth, unnamed colour.
- "Immediately right of" means the next position in the sequence house1 through house5.
- A person smokes one brand, drinks one beverage, and owns one pet.
- A zebra exists and belongs to a resident.

## Ontology issues that would block a reasoner from reaching the result

- **The `owns` range is still `:Dog`.** Clue 7 now makes this bite: a reasoner will infer that the Old Gold smoker's snails are dogs. Replace the range with an `Animal` class and make `Dog`, `Snail`, and `Zebra` disjoint.
- **Colours are only partly disjoint.** Only Red and Green are declared disjoint, so a house could be both red and yellow. That undoes the "Englishman isn't the Kools smoker" step.
- **Coffee and Tea are not disjoint.** The Ukrainian could therefore live in the green house.
- **Clue 6 has no positional effect.** `immediatelyRightOf` isn't functional, and no adjacency facts are asserted between house1 and house5.
- **Nothing makes values unique.** There are no uniqueness axioms for persons, nationalities, brands, or pets. `AllDifferent` covers only the houses.
- **The header is stale.** It still describes the file as the initial clue set.
