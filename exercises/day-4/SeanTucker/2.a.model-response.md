## What the ontology contains

- **Clue 2:** The Englishman lives in the red house.
- **Clue 3:** The Spaniard owns the dog.
- **Clue 4:** Coffee is drunk in the green house.
- **Clue 5:** The Ukrainian drinks tea.
- **Structure:** There are five distinct houses, and each has exactly one colour and one resident.

There is no `Zebra` class, no zebra individual, and no clue that mentions a zebra.

## Reasoning

1. **Eliminate the Spaniard.** He owns the dog. Ruling him out assumes each person owns exactly one pet. The ontology doesn't say this; clue 3 only says he owns *some* dog.
2. **The Englishman and the Ukrainian are unconstrained.** Their clues concern a house colour and a drink, not pets. Nothing favours one over the other.
3. **Two residents are unaccounted for.** There are five houses with one resident each, but only three nationalities are named. The other two people could own the zebra just as plausibly.
4. **Result:** With a uniform prior over the four non-Spaniard residents, "unnamed nationality" gets 2/4 of the probability, and English and Ukrainian get 1/4 each.

## Assumptions (none are stated in the file)

- There are five people, each with a distinct nationality. The ontology has no `AllDifferent` on persons and doesn't say nationality roles are unique.
- Each person owns exactly one pet, and each pet has one owner. `owns` has no cardinality or functional constraint.
- A zebra exists and has an owner. Under OWL's open-world assumption, neither follows.

## An ontology issue worth flagging

`:owns` has `rdfs:range :Dog`. Under OWL semantics, **anything owned is inferred to be a Dog**. If you asserted `:someone :owns :zebra1`, a reasoner would classify `zebra1` as a Dog, and nothing would contradict that, since there's no `Zebra` class and no disjointness. As written, the ontology can't represent zebra ownership correctly. You'd want a general `Animal` range, `Zebra ⊑ Animal`, and `Zebra disjointWith Dog`.

Some minor points:

- `Coffee` and `Tea` aren't declared disjoint.
- `Color` has only two subclasses and no covering axiom.
- `CoffeeDrinking` and `TeaDrinking` have a redundant `subClassOf :Drinking` alongside their equivalence axioms.
