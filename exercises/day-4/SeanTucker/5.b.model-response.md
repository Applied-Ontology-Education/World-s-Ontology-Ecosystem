## Solution (under the standard assumptions)

**Assumptions I used:**
- Each category has five distinct values, one per house, and each value is used exactly once.
- The unnamed fifth pet is the zebra and the unnamed fifth drink is water.
- `nextTo` means the two houses are adjacent in positions 1–5.
- "Right" means the higher-numbered house.

**Deduction chain:**
1. The Norwegian lives in house 1 (clue 10), so the blue house is house 2 (clue 15).
2. Milk is drunk in house 3 (clue 9). Green must sit immediately right of ivory, and green is where coffee is drunk, so green and ivory can only be houses 4–5 (not 3–4). Green is house 5 and ivory is house 4.
3. Red is house 3, home of the Englishman, so yellow is house 1. That puts the Kools smoker in house 1 (clue 8) and the horse in house 2 (clue 12).
4. The remaining clues fill in the rest:

| House | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | yellow | blue | red | ivory | green |
| Nationality | Norwegian | Ukrainian | English | Spanish | **Japanese** |
| Drink | *water* | tea | milk | orange juice | coffee |
| Smoke | Kools | Chesterfields | Old Gold | Lucky Strike | Parliaments |
| Pet | fox | horse | snails | dog | ***zebra*** |

I confirmed this by exhaustive search: exactly one solution exists. When I reversed "right" to mean the lower-numbered house, the search again found exactly one solution, and it still gives the zebra to the Japanese resident.

**Confidence:** Very high (about 99%) that the Japanese resident owns the zebra, given the assumptions above. The remaining doubt comes from reading "the zebra" as the unnamed fifth pet, which the ontology never states. If you rely on the ontology alone, as a reasoner would, the answer can't be derived.

## Fixes needed for a reasoner to reach this

1. Add `Pet ⊑ bfo:object`, make `Dog`, `Snail`, `Fox`, `Horse` and `Zebra` subclasses of `Pet` and pairwise disjoint, and set the range of `owns` to `Pet`. Add `Water`.
2. Declare pairwise disjointness within colours, nationality roles, beverages and brands.
3. Add the missing cardinalities: `livesIn` functional; exactly one nationality, drink, brand and pet per person; and uniqueness per value, most simply by using individuals (`:red`, `:english`, …) with `AllDifferent` plus functional and inverse-functional properties.
4. Assert `immediatelyRightOf` facts (house2 → house1, and so on), make it functional, and make it a sub-property of `nextTo`. Close `nextTo`, for example with negative property assertions or a maximum of 2 neighbours.
5. Model persons as five named, distinct individuals.

**In plain terms:** Once all the clues are applied with the usual "one of everything per house" rules, only one arrangement works, and in it the Japanese person in the green house owns the zebra. The file isn't ready for automated reasoning yet: it never mentions a zebra or water, it still treats every owned animal as a dog, and it doesn't say that each colour, drink or pet belongs to just one house. A reasoner running on it today couldn't reach this answer.
